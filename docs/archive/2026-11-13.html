Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO after affair scandal, CFO Kress steps in

The only chief executive Nvidia has ever had quits after a board probe into multiple alleged affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy.

Jensen Huang has resigned as chief executive of Nvidia, the chipmaker he co-founded in 1993 and built into the world's most valuable AI hardware company. Nvidia's board met on Wednesday night after reports surfaced of a second alleged affair, this time with an executive who reported directly to Huang, and announced his departure the next morning. Chief financial officer Colette Kress has been installed as interim CEO while the board, now chaired for the first time by Harvey Jones, continues its investigation.

The scandal began with Huang's acknowledged relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at AI compute firm Megaspeed who US intelligence reportedly identified as working for China's Ministry of State Security. A second alleged affair, with Nvidia vice president Kimberly Powell, reportedly involved a non-disclosure agreement being sought from a bystander who recorded the pair together. Reuters later reported a third alleged relationship with another Nvidia employee, and the stock closed down 4.3 percent on the news.

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer says Huang must still face Congress to explain what national security risks Nvidia and the Trump administration knew about. Huang had made himself central to US AI policy, including talks over Chinese access to advanced chips, and had no succession plan in place. President Trump has already floated former Apple boss Tim Cook as a replacement, while reaction from tech leaders has split between calling the resignation an overreaction and a necessary response to a serious conflict of interest.

Sources: The Verge · The Verge · NVIDIA Newsroom

Key concepts in this story
Market capitalisation
This is the total value of a company's shares, calculated by multiplying share price by the number of shares outstanding. Nvidia's market cap has topped $4 trillion, making it one of the most valuable companies ever.
Interim CEO
An interim CEO temporarily runs a company, often during a crisis or search for a permanent replacement, without the board committing to keep them long-term.
Congressional oversight
Reminder: this is when lawmakers question or investigate companies and government agencies to check they're acting legally and safely, as Schumer wants to do with Huang.
NDA (non-disclosure agreement)
Reminder: a legal contract that stops someone sharing certain information, often used to keep embarrassing or sensitive details private.
Why it matters for you

Governance, compliance and investor-relations roles suddenly matter a lot at AI companies, and the episode is a reminder that even dominant firms are vulnerable to leadership shocks. In interviews, being able to discuss succession planning and board oversight shows you think beyond the technology itself.

Cisco shares jump as AI infrastructure revenue guidance doubles to $9bn

The networking giant, late to the AI boom, says hyperscaler demand for its gear will roughly double this fiscal year.

Cisco's shares rose after the company beat Wall Street's expectations in its fiscal first quarter, reporting revenue of $18.5 billion against forecasts of $18.1 billion, and adjusted earnings per share of $1.40 versus the $1.33 expected. Revenue was up 24 percent on the year, while net income more than doubled to $4.6 billion.

Cisco has spent much of the generative AI boom playing catch-up to chipmakers and cloud providers, but it now says demand from hyperscalers, the giant cloud companies like Microsoft and Amazon that run vast data centres, is accelerating. The firm raised its guidance for AI infrastructure revenue to $9 billion this fiscal year, more than double what it made last year. CEO Chuck Robbins said customers are turning to Cisco to help them "scale, secure and optimise" their networks for what he called the "agentic AI era."

For the current quarter, Cisco is forecasting revenue of $18 billion to $18.2 billion and adjusted earnings per share of $1.34 to $1.36, both ahead of analyst estimates. The results land on a day when the AI industry's biggest name, Nvidia, is dealing with a leadership crisis, underlining that the AI buildout has many beneficiaries beyond chipmakers.

Sources: Tech

Key concepts in this story
Earnings per share (EPS)
This is a company's profit divided by its number of shares outstanding, used to judge how profitable a company is per share owned. If a firm earns $140 million profit and has 100 million shares, EPS is $1.40.
Hyperscaler
A hyperscaler is a company that runs enormous cloud computing operations, like Microsoft, Amazon or Google, with data centres spanning the globe. They're the biggest buyers of AI chips and networking equipment.
AI agent
Reminder: an AI agent is software that can take multi-step actions on its own, like booking a flight or managing a workflow, rather than just answering a single question.
Why it matters for you

Networking, security and infrastructure roles are becoming just as important to the AI boom as chip design, so it's worth knowing Cisco's pivot when discussing where AI job growth is happening beyond the obvious names.

Cerebras stock surges 17% on new Microsoft deal and tripled revenue forecast

The Nvidia challenger beat earnings estimates and says a newly signed Microsoft partnership will start generating revenue next year.

Cerebras Systems' shares jumped 17 percent in after-hours trading after the company posted better-than-expected quarterly results and unveiled a new deal with Microsoft. Core revenue rose 70 percent year-on-year to $230.9 million, beating the $214.7 million analysts had expected, while the company posted an adjusted profit of 12 cents per share against a forecast loss.

CEO Andrew Feldman said long lead times and high prices for Nvidia's graphics processing units (GPUs) have created an opening for Cerebras' alternative hardware. The Microsoft deal, signed in October, is expected to start showing up in revenue in the first quarter of 2027, adding to existing partnerships with Amazon and Oracle. Cerebras said it is now evaluating financing options to keep up with demand.

Looking further ahead, Cerebras forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $310 million to $330 million with a gross margin of 47 to 49 percent, both ahead of estimates, and said it expects 2027 revenue to triple to roughly $2.85 billion. Even so, the stock remains down about 47 percent since its Nasdaq debut in May, a reminder that beating one quarter's numbers doesn't erase broader investor doubts about the AI chip race. The results landed on the same day Nvidia's Jensen Huang resigned, a coincidence that only sharpened attention on who might challenge Nvidia next.

Sources: Tech

Key concepts in this story
Gross margin
Gross margin is the percentage of revenue left after subtracting the direct cost of making a product. If a chip sells for $100 and costs $50 to make, gross margin is 50 percent.
Wafer-scale chip
Most computer chips are small squares cut from a round silicon wafer, but Cerebras makes chips the size of an entire wafer, aiming for faster performance on certain AI workloads.
GPU vs CPU
Reminder: a GPU (graphics processing unit) can do many simple calculations at once, making it well suited to AI, while a CPU (central processing unit) handles tasks one after another and is more general-purpose.
Why it matters for you

Watching how smaller chip challengers like Cerebras win hyperscaler deals helps you understand that the AI hardware market isn't a one-horse race, which is a useful point to raise if asked in an interview about competition to Nvidia.

Anthropic's IPO oversubscribed nearly six times as Wall Street tests AI appetite

The ChatGPT rival's stock market debut arrives just as Nvidia's leadership crisis rattles confidence in AI investing.

Anthropic's initial public offering has reportedly been oversubscribed by nearly six times, suggesting strong investor demand for shares in the AI lab behind the Claude chatbot. The listing is being closely watched as a test of whether public markets still have an appetite for AI stocks after a volatile few weeks.

Commentators have reportedly asked whether Anthropic could become "the new Nvidia" for investors seeking AI exposure, though the comparison comes amid a broader stock market pullback in the sector. The timing has been complicated further by Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation as Nvidia's chief executive, which has added fresh uncertainty to how investors view AI-related listings right now.

Sources: The Economic Times · Livemint · Fortune

Key concepts in this story
Oversubscribed
Reminder: an IPO is oversubscribed when investors want to buy more shares than are actually being offered, a sign of strong demand that can push the price up on debut.
IPO (initial public offering)
Reminder: an IPO is the first time a private company sells shares to the public on a stock exchange, letting ordinary investors buy in.
AI bubble
Reminder: this is the worry that AI company valuations have risen faster than their actual profits justify, meaning prices could fall sharply if expectations aren't met.
Why it matters for you

If you're interested in equity research or investment banking, Anthropic's IPO is a live case study in how to judge whether a hot sector's valuations are justified by real revenue.

Alphabet draws $7bn in orders for Hong Kong and Singapore bond sale to fund AI build-out

Google's parent company reportedly tapped Asian debt markets as it keeps spending heavily on AI infrastructure.

Alphabet has reportedly drawn around $7 billion in investor orders for a bond sale in Hong Kong and Singapore, as the Google parent taps Asian debt markets to help fund its ongoing AI spending. The move adds to a wave of borrowing by big tech companies seeking cash for data centres and chips without having to sell more shares.

Sources: Simply Wall St

Key concepts in this story
Bond
Reminder: a bond is a loan investors make to a company or government, which pays back the amount borrowed plus interest over an agreed period.
Capital expenditure (capex)
Reminder: capex is money a company spends on long-term assets like buildings, equipment or data centres, rather than day-to-day running costs.
Debt market
A debt market is where companies and governments sell bonds to investors to raise money, as an alternative to issuing shares.
Why it matters for you

Big tech's growing reliance on debt to fund AI spending is a key thread connecting today's stories about circular financing and balance sheet risk across the industry.

ECB: AI investment now drives a quarter of euro zone credit growth

Europe's central bank says lending tied to AI projects has become a significant driver of new borrowing across the bloc.

The European Central Bank has reportedly said that AI-related investment now accounts for roughly a quarter of all new credit growth across the euro area. The finding highlights how deeply AI spending, from data centres to chips, is becoming woven into the wider European banking and lending system, not just confined to a handful of tech giants.

Sources: The Economic Times

Key concepts in this story
Central bank
Reminder: a central bank, like the European Central Bank, manages a currency and oversees the banking system, including tracking how fast lending across the economy is growing.
Credit growth
Credit growth measures how much more borrowing is happening across an economy compared to before, which can signal rising investment or rising risk.
AI bubble
Reminder: the worry that AI-linked valuations and lending have outpaced the actual profits being generated, meaning a slowdown could hit banks as well as tech firms.
Why it matters for you

When central banks start tracking AI's share of credit growth, it shows regulators are watching for systemic risk, a theme worth mentioning if asked about regulation in a banking interview.

Startups & funding

LangChain more than doubles valuation to $3bn in $300m round led by CapitalG

The popular framework for building AI agents raised fresh funding from Alphabet's growth-equity arm.

LangChain, the startup behind a widely used open-source framework for building AI agents, has reportedly raised $300 million in a round that more than doubles its valuation to $3 billion. The round was led by CapitalG, the growth-equity investment arm of Alphabet, Google's parent company.

LangChain's tools help developers chain together calls to large language models, giving chatbots memory, access to external data and the ability to take multi-step actions. The scale of this round, more than doubling its valuation in a short period, shows how much investor money is still chasing companies that sit underneath the big AI labs rather than building their own models from scratch.

Sources: TradingView · WSJ · MLQ.ai

Key concepts in this story
Funding round
Reminder: a funding round is when a startup sells a stake in itself to investors in exchange for cash, usually named by stage such as Series A or Series C.
Valuation
Reminder: a valuation is what investors agree a company is worth, usually estimated from the price paid for a small stake multiplied up to the whole company.
Open source
Open-source software has its underlying code made publicly available for anyone to use, modify or build on, which is how LangChain built such a large developer following.
Why it matters for you

LangChain's rise shows that tooling for AI agents, not just the models themselves, is becoming a huge business, useful to know if you're considering roles building on top of LLMs rather than training them.

Startups & funding

Mirendil raises $1bn Series A to fund frontier AI research

The unusually large early-stage round reflects investors' willingness to write giant cheques for AI labs before they've built a track record.

Startup Mirendil has reportedly raised $1 billion in a Series A funding round aimed at bringing frontier AI research and development capabilities to more teams. A round of this size is highly unusual at the Series A stage, which typically involves far smaller sums for young companies still proving their idea works.

The scale of the raise underlines how much capital investors are prepared to commit early to companies positioning themselves as frontier AI labs, even before widespread public details about products or customers.

Sources: Unite.AI · Howl.Link

Key concepts in this story
Series A funding
Reminder: Series A is typically a startup's first major round of venture funding after smaller seed investment, usually used to build out a product and team.
Frontier AI lab
Reminder: a frontier AI lab is a company trying to build the most advanced, cutting-edge AI models, such as OpenAI, Anthropic or Google DeepMind.
Mega-round
A mega-round is an unusually large funding round, often hundreds of millions or billions of dollars, far above what's typical for a startup at that stage.
Why it matters for you

Billion-dollar Series A rounds show investors betting heavily on unproven AI labs, a trend worth flagging in any discussion about whether startup valuations are getting ahead of actual results.

Code corner

Projecting Cerebras' revenue growth with compound annual growth rate

Based on: Cerebras stock surges 17% on new Microsoft deal and tripled revenue forecast

Cerebras said it expects 2027 revenue to roughly triple. Let's use Python to check what growth rate that implies and project a few years forward.

def project_revenue(start_revenue, growth_rate, years):
    results = []
    revenue = start_revenue
    for year in range(1, years + 1):
        revenue = revenue * (1 + growth_rate)
        results.append((year, round(revenue, 1)))
    return results

# Cerebras 2026 guidance midpoint (approx, in $ millions)
revenue_2026 = (310 + 330) / 2 * 4  # rough annualised estimate

# Implied growth rate to triple by 2027
triple_growth_rate = 3 ** (1/1) - 1  # one year to triple

projection = project_revenue(revenue_2026, triple_growth_rate, 3)

print(f"Estimated 2026 revenue: ${revenue_2026:,.1f}m")
for year, rev in projection:
    print(f"Year {year}: ${rev:,.1f}m")
Line by line
  1. def project_revenue(...): defines a reusable function that compounds revenue growth year by year, like interest compounding on savings.
  2. revenue = revenue * (1 + growth_rate) increases revenue by a fixed percentage each year, the same maths used for compound interest.
  3. revenue_2026 estimates a rough annual figure by averaging Cerebras' quarterly guidance and multiplying by four quarters.
  4. triple_growth_rate works out what single-year growth rate would be needed to triple revenue, using Python's exponent operator **.
  5. The for loop at the bottom calls the function and prints out a simple year-by-year projection table.

Try it: Change growth_rate to model a slower scenario, such as revenue doubling instead of tripling, by replacing '3 ** (1/1) - 1' with '2 ** (1/1) - 1', and see how the projected figures change.
