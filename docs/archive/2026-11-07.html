The AI industry

OpenAI reportedly raises $11bn at a $1.25 trillion valuation

The ChatGPT maker's valuation has jumped by roughly 47% in eight months, even as rival Anthropic's IPO ambitions wobble.

OpenAI has reportedly raised $11 billion as part of a new funding round that values the company at $1.25 trillion, according to Bloomberg. The round has not yet closed and could eventually total around $20 billion once complete, a figure that falls short of the $30 billion the company was said to be targeting back in September.

The jump is striking: OpenAI raised $122 billion in March at an $852 billion valuation. A funding round is simply investors agreeing to buy a slice of a private company at an agreed price, which in turn sets a paper value for the whole business, not cash sitting in the bank. OpenAI has previously signalled it could go public, an IPO, but has pushed those plans back, with most expectations now pointing to 2027.

The timing is notable because reports suggest potential buyers in rival Anthropic's upcoming IPO are eyeing a valuation as low as $1.1 trillion, well below the $2 trillion once expected. That gap hints at cooling investor enthusiasm across the sector.

Adding to the uncertainty, the US midterm elections have produced a wave of negative public sentiment towards data centres, raising the prospect of new regulatory hurdles for the AI industry just as the biggest players are asking investors for ever larger sums.

Sources: TechCrunch · The Verge · Українські Національні Новини (УНН)

Key concepts in this story
Funding round
A reminder: this is when a company sells new shares to investors in exchange for cash, often in named stages such as Series A, B or C.
Valuation
A reminder: this is the price tag investors put on a whole company based on what they're willing to pay for a small slice of it.
IPO (initial public offering)
A reminder: this is when a private company sells shares to the public for the first time, listing on a stock exchange.
Why it matters for you

Huge valuations like this fuel hiring in AI research, infrastructure and finance roles that support fundraising and compliance. If you're interviewing at an AI company, understanding that a valuation is a negotiated price, not cash on hand, shows financial literacy beyond the hype.

The AI industry

Microsoft reportedly winds down deal to rent AI cloud capacity from Oracle

Bloomberg reports Microsoft will not renew a 2023 agreement, raising questions about Oracle's huge contract backlog.

Microsoft is reportedly winding down a 2023 deal under which it rented AI computing capacity from Oracle, according to Bloomberg, with some reports suggesting older chips in the arrangement no longer met Microsoft's needs.

Oracle has built up a contract backlog worth hundreds of billions of dollars from AI-related cloud deals, a figure investors have been watching closely as a sign of future revenue. News that one of its largest customers may be stepping back triggered a share price drop for Oracle.

The episode highlights how quickly compute capacity deals, essentially long-term rental agreements for data centre hardware, can shift as AI companies reassess which chips and providers best serve their needs.

Sources: Bloomberg.com · Reuters · The Next Web

Key concepts in this story
Contract backlog
This is the total value of signed deals a company hasn't yet delivered or billed for; a big backlog suggests strong future revenue, but only if customers don't cancel.
Compute capacity
This refers to the processing power - chips, servers and data centre space - needed to train or run AI models, which companies often rent rather than buy outright.
Why it matters for you

Cloud contracts between tech giants shape demand for data centre jobs and chip supply chains; spotting when a backlog might not convert to revenue is a useful skill for anyone analysing tech company earnings.

US officials reportedly believe Nvidia's Jensen Huang had relationship with alleged Chinese spy

The Financial Times reports US authorities suspect a personal link between Nvidia's chief executive and an alleged intelligence operative.

The Financial Times has reported that US officials believe Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with a person alleged to be a Chinese spy. Details beyond the initial report remain limited.

Nvidia's chips are central to the global AI boom and are subject to export controls restricting sales to China over national security concerns. Any suggestion of a personal connection between Huang and alleged Chinese intelligence activity would add a new layer of scrutiny to a company already under close government watch.

Sources: Financial Times

Key concepts in this story
Export controls
A reminder: these are government rules restricting the sale of sensitive technology, like advanced AI chips, to certain countries for national security reasons.
National security review
This is when government agencies examine a company, deal or individual for risks to a country's safety or strategic interests, which can lead to new restrictions or investigations.
Why it matters for you

Nvidia sits at the centre of AI hardware supply chains, so any political or security scrutiny of its leadership could ripple through chip exports, data centre building plans and investor confidence.

AI & finance

CoreWeave bond yield tops 16% as AI debt gets pricier

Rising borrowing costs for the AI cloud provider suggest investors are demanding a bigger premium for AI infrastructure risk.

CNBC reports that yields on bonds issued by CoreWeave, a major AI cloud computing provider, have climbed above 16%. A bond's yield moves opposite to its price, so a sharply rising yield signals investors are selling the debt or demanding much higher compensation for the risk of lending to the company.

CoreWeave is one of the so-called neoclouds that rents out GPU computing power to AI labs, often financed heavily with debt secured against its chips. Climbing yields on that debt suggest some investors are growing nervous about whether AI infrastructure spending will keep paying off as expected.

Sources: CNBC

Key concepts in this story
Bond yield
This is the effective return a bond investor earns; if a $100 bond paying $5 a year falls in price to $80, its yield rises because that same $5 is now a bigger percentage return.
Credit spread
A reminder: this is the extra interest a risky borrower must pay compared with a safe government bond, and it widens when investors see more risk of default.
Why it matters for you

Bond yields are an early warning signal in finance; understanding why a rising yield can mean falling confidence is essential for anyone analysing lending risk in fast-growing but debt-heavy sectors like AI infrastructure.

AI & finance

ING puts $500m of data centre loans up for sale, including Stargate site debt

The Dutch bank is reportedly auctioning loans tied to AI data centres, among them a slice of OpenAI's Stargate project.

ING Bank is reportedly looking to sell around $500 million of loans linked to data centres in a single auction, including debt connected to OpenAI's Stargate data centre site, according to BeBeez International and MLQ.ai.

Selling loans on to other investors, known as the secondary market, lets banks free up money to lend elsewhere while spreading out the risk of financing enormous AI data centre projects. It also signals how much bank balance sheets are now tied up in AI infrastructure lending.

Sources: BeBeez International · MLQ.ai

Key concepts in this story
Loan sale (secondary market)
A reminder: this is when a bank sells an existing loan to another investor, transferring the right to receive repayments along with the risk of default.
Data centre
A reminder: these are large facilities packed with servers and chips that power AI training and everyday cloud services, and they are extremely expensive to build and finance.
Why it matters for you

Banks reselling data centre debt shows how AI infrastructure risk is spreading across the financial system, a trend worth understanding if you want to work in credit analysis or risk management.

AI & finance

Goldman and Apollo reportedly shop slices of SpaceX's $40bn Nvidia chip lease

Investment banks are said to be offering investors pieces of a huge chip-leasing deal tied to SpaceX.

Goldman Sachs and Apollo are reportedly marketing slices of a $40 billion lease arrangement involving Nvidia chips used by SpaceX, according to MLQ.ai. Breaking a large loan or lease into smaller pieces and selling them to multiple investors is a way of spreading risk and raising more capital than any single lender might want to hold alone.

The deal underscores how deeply AI chip financing now reaches beyond pure tech companies into aerospace and defence-adjacent firms, and how banks are packaging and reselling that debt much as they do with other large corporate loans.

Sources: MLQ.ai

Key concepts in this story
Securitisation
This is the process of bundling a loan or lease into smaller tradeable pieces that different investors can buy, similar to slicing a pizza so several people can each have a share.
GPU-backed loan
A reminder: this is when a company borrows money using its graphics processing units, the chips that power AI computing, as collateral.
Why it matters for you

Seeing AI chip deals packaged like mortgage debt is a reminder that financial engineering, not just AI breakthroughs, is driving the industry's growth, which matters if you're weighing a career in structured finance or risk analysis.

Startups & funding

Palo Alto Networks in advanced talks to buy AI agent security startup Clutch for $500m

The cybersecurity giant is reportedly close to acquiring a startup that protects AI agents from misuse.

Palo Alto Networks is reportedly in advanced talks to acquire Clutch Security, a startup focused on securing AI agents, for around $500 million, according to multiple outlets including Security Boulevard and SC Media.

AI agents, software that can take actions on a user's behalf such as making purchases or querying databases, increasingly need their own credentials and access permissions, much like human employees. Startups like Clutch build tools to manage and secure those non-human identities, a growing concern as companies deploy more autonomous AI systems.

Sources: Techzine Global · Security Boulevard · The American Bazaar

Key concepts in this story
AI agent
A reminder: this is software that can carry out multi-step tasks on its own, such as booking a flight or querying a database, rather than just answering a single question.
Acquisition
A reminder: this is when one company buys another outright, often to gain its technology, team or customers quickly rather than building it in-house.
Why it matters for you

As companies roll out AI agents, demand is rising fast for cybersecurity specialists who understand how to manage machine identities and permissions, a niche worth knowing about if you're considering a security career.

Startups & funding

Sentra raises $150m Series C led by Capital One Ventures

The data security startup's new funding round was led by the venture arm of a major US bank.

Sentra, a data security startup, has raised $150 million in a Series C funding round led by Capital One Ventures, according to Beinsure. Series C typically marks a later-stage round for startups that have already proven their product works and are now scaling sales and operations, as opposed to earlier seed or Series A rounds focused on getting a product off the ground.

The involvement of Capital One's venture arm highlights how banks are increasingly investing directly in startups building tools, like data security products, that they themselves may want to use or that protect sensitive financial data.

Sources: Beinsure

Key concepts in this story
Series C funding
This is a later stage of startup fundraising, usually once a company has paying customers and steady revenue, aimed at scaling the business rather than just building the first product.
Venture capital
This is money invested by specialist firms, or arms of larger companies, into early or growing startups in exchange for equity, betting that some will become very valuable.
Why it matters for you

Banks investing in security startups shows where financial institutions see future risk, useful context if you're applying for roles in fintech partnerships or corporate venture teams.

Code corner

Calculating a bond's current yield

Based on: CoreWeave bond yield tops 16% as AI debt gets pricier

CoreWeave's bond yield has reportedly climbed above 16% as its price falls. Here's a simple script that shows how a bond's price and yield are linked, using only Python's standard library.

def current_yield(face_value, coupon_rate, market_price):
    annual_coupon = face_value * coupon_rate
    yield_pct = (annual_coupon / market_price) * 100
    return round(yield_pct, 2)

face_value = 100
coupon_rate = 0.08  # 8% annual coupon

prices = [100, 90, 70, 50]

for price in prices:
    y = current_yield(face_value, coupon_rate, price)
    print(f"Price: ${price} -> Current yield: {y}%")
Line by line
  1. def current_yield(...) defines a function that works out a bond's yield from its face value, coupon rate and current market price.
  2. annual_coupon = face_value * coupon_rate calculates the fixed dollar amount the bond pays each year, here $8 on a $100 bond with an 8% coupon.
  3. yield_pct = (annual_coupon / market_price) * 100 divides that fixed payment by the current price to get the yield as a percentage; as price falls, yield rises.
  4. The for loop runs the calculation for several falling prices, showing how a bond trading at $50 instead of $100 doubles its yield even though the coupon payment never changes.

Try it: Change the coupon_rate to 0.05 and add a price of $40 to the prices list, then run the script again to see how much higher the yield climbs for a deeply discounted bond.
