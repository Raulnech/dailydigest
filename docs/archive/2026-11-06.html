The AI industry

43 of 44 data centre moratoria pass as Democrats sweep midterms

A blue wave in Tuesday's elections has turned local opposition to AI data centres into a national political force.

Democrats took control of the House and Senate on Tuesday night in a sweeping set of midterm results, and across the country voters also delivered a clear verdict on AI infrastructure: 43 out of 44 local ballot measures to halt or restrict data centre construction passed. A ballot measure is a proposal that voters directly approve or reject, separate from candidates. In Texas, the heart of the AI data centre boom, governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has promised a moratorium, a temporary ban, on new approvals until stronger laws are passed.

The backlash has been building for months. Residents near data centres have complained about rising electricity bills, strained water supplies and noise, and an NBC poll found 76% of voters opposed data centres in their own communities. Analysts and advocacy groups described the result as a bipartisan rejection of the industry's current approach, with campaigners in several states explicitly linking household bills to the tech companies driving demand for new power and water.

AI company bosses have mostly stayed quiet in public, but the Wall Street Journal reported that executives, including at OpenAI, have privately discussed real constraints on future computing capacity as a result. Some, including Meta's platforms president, are said to be weighing moving data centre construction overseas instead. With incoming lawmakers promising hearings and tighter state rules, the era of AI firms building data centres with little local pushback looks to be ending.

Sources: The Verge · Tech · Tech

Key concepts in this story
Ballot measure
A ballot measure lets voters directly approve or reject a specific policy, rather than just electing a politician. Dozens of US towns used them this week to vote on whether new data centres could be built.
Regulatory risk
This is the danger that new laws or rules could hurt a company's plans or profits. For AI firms, the risk just jumped because incoming lawmakers have pledged tougher oversight of data centre expansion.
Data centre (reminder)
A data centre is a large building full of computer servers, used here to train and run AI models. They need huge amounts of electricity and water for cooling, which is why communities are pushing back.
Why it matters for you

If you want to work in AI policy, infrastructure planning or ESG analysis, this is the story to know: companies will increasingly need people who can navigate community relations and local regulation, not just build bigger data centres. It's also a useful interview talking point about how political risk is becoming as important as technical risk in AI.

The AI industry

OpenAI drops NDAs for data centre deals after election rebuke

Days after voters punished data centre expansion at the ballot box, OpenAI says it will stop using non-disclosure agreements with public officials.

OpenAI announced on Friday that it will no longer use non-disclosure agreements, or NDAs, with public officials when negotiating data centre projects. An NDA is a legal contract that stops someone sharing certain information; critics say developers have used them to keep details of local deals hidden from residents. OpenAI says it will now only protect narrow categories such as genuine security information or trade secrets.

The move follows this week's midterm elections, in which voters across the country backed measures to restrict or pause new data centre construction. Amazon made a similar pledge to drop NDAs with government agencies back in October. OpenAI's new commitments also include paying the full cost of the electricity and infrastructure its data centres use, publishing expected job numbers, and reporting on hiring in host communities.

Every future data centre that OpenAI owns and operates — which it has rebranded as a 'compute campus' — will come with a 'local compact' reflecting these commitments and local priorities. The announcement is a clear attempt to defuse the kind of community anger that fuelled Tuesday's political backlash, though it remains to be seen whether voluntary pledges will satisfy lawmakers who are already planning hearings and new state rules.

Sources: The Verge · Tech

Key concepts in this story
NDA (non-disclosure agreement)
An NDA is a signed promise not to reveal certain information to others. Data centre developers have used them with local officials, which made it hard for residents to know what deals were struck on their behalf.
Community benefits agreement
This is a formal commitment a company makes to a local area in exchange for building something there, such as paying for infrastructure or creating jobs. OpenAI's new 'local compact' is an example.
Why it matters for you

This shows how political pressure can force tech giants to change their business practices almost overnight — a pattern worth citing if you're asked in an interview how companies manage regulatory and reputational risk around AI.

The AI industry

China's Moonshot AI seeks Nvidia Blackwell access via European data centres

Export controls have pushed the Chinese AI lab to look for ways to rent advanced Nvidia chips hosted outside China.

Chinese AI lab Moonshot AI has held talks this year to lease computing capacity from European data centre operators, known as neoclouds, that host Nvidia's advanced Blackwell chips, according to sources cited by CNBC. US export controls ban the direct sale of these chips to China, but accessing them remotely through cloud services based overseas is not currently covered by the rules, creating what amounts to a loophole.

Moonshot, maker of the popular Kimi models, was reportedly interested in clusters of 8,000 or more Blackwell GPUs (graphics processing units, the chips best suited to AI work) for both running models for users, known as inference, and training new ones. One source said the company had been described as 'very aggressive' in chasing more computing power, and some sources said Moonshot had been willing to pay 30–50% more upfront than a typical customer to secure a deal. This follows a July incident in which the White House said Moonshot had accessed Blackwell chips remotely via a facility in Thailand.

Washington has proposed legislation, the Remote Access Security Act, to close this loophole by extending export controls to cloud-based chip access, but it has stalled in the Senate. Nvidia has reportedly told cloud providers that at least 51% of their chip capacity must go to non-Chinese customers. With Chinese models like Kimi gaining ground globally on cost and capability, more compute access for Moonshot would intensify competition for US labs such as OpenAI and Anthropic.

Sources: Tech · Yahoo Finance UK

Key concepts in this story
Export control evasion (reminder)
This is when a company finds a legal workaround to get restricted technology despite trade bans. Here, renting foreign-hosted chips instead of buying them directly sidesteps US rules on selling chips to China.
Offtake agreement
An offtake agreement is a deal where a customer agrees in advance to buy a set amount of a product or service, often paying a fee upfront to secure it. Moonshot reportedly offered to pay well above the usual rate to lock in chip capacity.
Inference (reminder)
Inference is when a trained AI model is actually used to generate answers for users, as opposed to training, which builds the model in the first place. Moonshot wanted chip capacity for both.
Why it matters for you

Export control policy is becoming central to the AI industry's geography and economics — understanding loopholes like remote cloud access is useful if you're interested in trade policy, compliance or semiconductor supply chain roles.

Mystery seller tries to offload $500 million of data centre loans

ING is reportedly seeking buyers for half a billion dollars of loans tied to AI data centres, fuelling concern about debt backing the AI boom.

Reports from CNBC and Bloomberg say Dutch bank ING is looking for buyers to take $500 million of loans linked to data centre projects off its books. The identity of the original borrower and the exact reasons for the sale have not been disclosed, and the bank has not commented publicly.

Loans like these are often sold on by banks to spread out risk, a process known as syndication. But a sale of this size being shopped around now is being read by some analysts as a sign that investors are growing more cautious about debt tied to the AI infrastructure build-out, much of which has been financed with large amounts of borrowed money.

With so much AI expansion funded by debt rather than cash, any sign of lenders wanting to reduce their exposure is being watched closely as an early indicator of how comfortable the market really is with AI-related risk.

Sources: CNBC · Bloomberg.com · Demokratis.co.id

Key concepts in this story
Syndicated loan (reminder)
A syndicated loan is one big loan split between several lenders to share the risk, like a group of banks each lending part of a $500m sum rather than one bank lending it all.
Loan sale (secondary market)
This is when a lender sells an existing loan to another investor instead of holding onto it until it's repaid. It lets banks free up cash or offload risk they're no longer comfortable holding.

RBC: hedge funds were 'massive' sellers of AI debt

A senior RBC analyst says hedge funds have been aggressively selling debt tied to AI infrastructure.

According to Bloomberg, RBC's Skiba said hedge funds have been 'massive' sellers of debt linked to AI projects recently. No further detail on volumes or specific borrowers was given in the available report.

Hedge funds are investment funds that often trade more actively and take on more risk than typical banks or pension funds, so heavy selling by them can be an early warning sign that sentiment toward a type of debt is souring, even before wider markets react.

Sources: Bloomberg.com

Key concepts in this story
Hedge fund
A hedge fund is a pooled investment fund that uses varied, often aggressive strategies to try to make money for wealthy or institutional investors. Unlike many investors, they can bet on prices falling as well as rising.
Credit spread (reminder)
This is the extra interest rate investors demand to hold riskier debt compared with safe government debt. Heavy selling of AI debt can push these spreads wider, making borrowing more expensive.

Banks brace for tighter oversight after Democrats' Congress sweep

Reuters reports banks are preparing for stricter scrutiny from a newly Democrat-controlled Congress.

Reuters reports that banks are bracing for tighter oversight now that Democrats have taken control of both chambers of Congress following this week's midterm elections. Details of specific new rules were not given in the available report.

Banking oversight typically covers how much capital banks must hold, how they lend, and how closely regulators watch for risky practices. A shift in Congress can change who leads key committees and how aggressively regulators enforce existing rules.

Sources: Reuters

Key concepts in this story
Banking regulation
This is the set of rules governments use to control how banks lend, how much risk they can take, and how much spare capital they must hold in case of losses. More oversight generally means banks must be more cautious.
Congressional oversight
This is Congress's power to question, investigate, and set rules for industries, including through hearings where executives can be called to testify.

Startups & funding

Shield AI to buy radar maker Echodyne for $5.3bn, hitting $28.8bn valuation

The autonomous combat drone maker's acquisition and new fundraise would make it one of the most valuable defence tech startups.

Shield AI, a maker of autonomous combat drones, is reportedly set to acquire radar startup Echodyne in a deal worth $5.3 billion, according to multiple reports including Bloomberg and SiliconANGLE. The acquisition is said to come alongside a fundraising round that would value Shield AI at $28.8 billion.

Echodyne makes radar technology, used to detect and track objects such as aircraft or drones, which would complement Shield AI's autonomous flight systems. Combining radar sensing with Shield AI's existing drone and software products points to a push toward a more complete, self-contained defence product line.

Details on the structure of the deal, investors involved, or timing have not been fully confirmed beyond the reported figures.

Sources: Bloomberg.com · SiliconANGLE · Startup Fortune

Key concepts in this story
Defence tech (reminder)
Defence tech refers to companies building technology for military or security use, such as drones, radar or surveillance systems, often selling to governments rather than consumers.
Dual-use technology (reminder)
This is technology that can be used for both civilian and military purposes, like radar, which can track weather or aircraft just as easily as it can track a drone. It's often subject to extra export restrictions.
Acquisition (reminder)
An acquisition is when one company buys another outright. Here Shield AI is reportedly paying $5.3bn to take over Echodyne.
Why it matters for you

Defence tech is becoming one of the hottest corners of the startup world, blending AI, hardware and government contracts — worth knowing if you're considering a career path outside consumer AI apps.

Startups & funding

Thinking Machines Lab nears $5bn round at $40bn valuation

The AI lab founded by former OpenAI chief technology officer Mira Murati is reportedly closing in on a huge new funding round.

Business Insider reports that Thinking Machines Lab is closing in on a new funding round of $5 billion, which would value the company at $40 billion. No further details on investors or timing were given in the available report.

The scale of the reported valuation underlines how much capital is still flowing into frontier AI labs — companies building the most advanced, general-purpose AI models — even as investors elsewhere grow more cautious about AI-linked debt and infrastructure spending.

Sources: Business Insider

Key concepts in this story
Funding round (reminder)
A funding round is when a company raises money from investors in exchange for a stake in the business. A $5bn round is an unusually large amount even by AI industry standards.
Frontier AI lab (reminder)
This refers to companies working at the cutting edge of AI capability, such as OpenAI, Anthropic or Thinking Machines Lab, as opposed to firms building smaller, specialised tools on top of existing models.
Why it matters for you

Huge valuations like this show investor appetite for frontier AI labs remains strong even as other parts of the AI financing stack, like data centre debt, show signs of strain — a useful contrast to raise if asked about AI bubble risk in an interview.

Code corner

Simulating an LLM sentiment check on today's headlines

Based on: 43 of 44 data centre moratoria pass as Democrats sweep midterms

News firms increasingly use LLMs to quickly tag headlines by sentiment or topic. Here's a simplified example using a fake API response, so it runs without needing a real key, inspired by today's AI-backlash story.

import json

# Pretend this came back from an LLM API call
fake_api_response = '''
{
  "headline": "43 of 44 data centre moratoria pass as Democrats sweep midterms",
  "sentiment": "negative",
  "topics": ["regulation", "data centres", "elections"],
  "tokens_used": 42
}
'''

def parse_llm_response(raw_json):
    data = json.loads(raw_json)
    return data

result = parse_llm_response(fake_api_response)

print("Headline:", result["headline"])
print("Sentiment:", result["sentiment"])
print("Topics:", ", ".join(result["topics"]))
print("Tokens used:", result["tokens_used"])

# Rough cost estimate: suppose $0.002 per 1000 tokens
cost_per_1k = 0.002
estimated_cost = (result["tokens_used"] / 1000) * cost_per_1k
print(f"Estimated cost: ${estimated_cost:.6f}")
Line by line
  1. `import json` loads Python's built-in module for reading and writing JSON, the text format most APIs use to send structured data.
  2. `fake_api_response` is a string written to look exactly like what a real LLM API would return, so we can practise parsing it without making a network call.
  3. `json.loads(raw_json)` turns that JSON string into a Python dictionary we can work with, like turning a sentence into labelled boxes of information.
  4. `result["sentiment"]` and similar lines simply pull out values from the dictionary by their key name.
  5. The final lines use `tokens_used`, a measure of how much text the model processed, to estimate roughly how much the API call would cost in real life.

Try it: Add a second fake headline to the JSON (as a list of headlines) and update the code to loop through each one, printing its sentiment and running total cost.
