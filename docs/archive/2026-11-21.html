Trump Threatens 'Tremendous Tax' on US Firms Building AI Data Centres Abroad

The president says American companies will be barred from building data centres overseas, even though tech giants already operate dozens of them.

President Donald Trump said on Friday that US companies will be stopped from building data centres in other countries and will face a 'tremendous tax' if they try. He made the announcement at a White House press conference, admitting afterwards that he hadn't even told his own staff about the plan beforehand.

The comments sit awkwardly alongside domestic opposition to the very data centres Trump wants to keep onshore. A CNBC survey found 64% of registered voters oppose having one built within ten miles of their home, against just 13% in favour. Meanwhile, cloud giants including Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Oracle already run data centres abroad, often to serve local customers faster or to comply with foreign data rules. Trump's remarks came a day after he met Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin at a trilateral summit in China, where Trump said Xi praised America's progress in what Trump called 'super intelligence'.

No company contacted for comment responded immediately, and it is unclear how or when any tax would be implemented. If enacted, it would force major cloud providers to choose between following US policy and meeting the needs of international clients who want nearby infrastructure for speed and regulatory reasons.

Sources: Tech · The Verge

Key concepts in this story
Tariff
A tariff is a tax a government places on goods or activities, often to discourage them. Here, Trump is proposing something similar for data centres built outside the US rather than physical goods crossing a border.
Hyperscaler
A hyperscaler is a company that runs enormous cloud computing operations at global scale, such as Amazon, Microsoft or Google. They build data centres worldwide so customers everywhere get fast, reliable access to computing power.
Data sovereignty
Data sovereignty means a country's rules about where its citizens' or companies' data must be stored and processed. It's one reason firms build data centres abroad, to keep data within a customer's home country as local law requires.
Why it matters for you

If this policy becomes real, it could reshape where cloud and AI infrastructure jobs are created, and it's a useful example in interviews of how politics, not just technology, drives AI industry decisions.

Nvidia Shares Sink 13% as SEC Probes Its China Accounting

Regulators are reportedly examining how Nvidia discloses revenue tied to Singapore and China amid wider scrutiny of the chipmaker's China exposure.

The US Securities and Exchange Commission has reportedly opened an investigation into Nvidia's accounting practices, according to multiple sources cited by Reuters and other outlets. The focus is said to be on how the company discloses revenue linked to Singapore, which has reportedly grown to nearly half of Nvidia's total sales.

Nvidia's shares fell 13% in a single trading day following the reports, reflecting investor nervousness about a company whose chips underpin much of the current AI boom. The scrutiny comes against a backdrop of broader concern about Nvidia's ties to China, a market complicated by US export restrictions on advanced chips.

Details remain limited, and Nvidia has not confirmed the scope or outcome of any review. Given Nvidia's outsized influence on AI-related stock indices, any formal findings could ripple through markets well beyond the company itself.

Sources: Reuters · Business Today · BigGo Finance

Key concepts in this story
SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission)
The SEC is the US government body that polices stock markets and company disclosures, making sure investors get accurate financial information. If it finds a company's accounting misleads investors, it can fine firms or refer cases for prosecution.
Revenue disclosure (geographic reporting)
Companies often have to break down where their sales come from, by country or region, in filings. A sudden change in how a company reports this, especially if one country's share balloons, can raise questions about why.
Short selling (reminder)
Short selling is betting a stock price will fall by borrowing shares, selling them, then buying them back cheaper later. Sharp drops like Nvidia's 13% fall often create profits for short sellers and losses for ordinary shareholders.
Why it matters for you

Accounting scrutiny of the AI industry's biggest chip supplier is a reminder that due diligence skills, reading disclosures and understanding regulatory risk, matter just as much in AI-adjacent finance roles as technical knowledge.

Anthropic Researcher Says AI Helped Crack 150-Year-Old Riemann Hypothesis

A mathematician at the AI lab claims a breakthrough on one of the most famous unsolved problems in maths, though peer review is still pending.

A researcher at Anthropic has reportedly claimed that an AI system helped solve the Riemann hypothesis, a problem about the distribution of prime numbers that has stumped mathematicians since the 1850s. The claim, reported by the BBC and the Wall Street Journal, would mark one of the most significant AI-assisted breakthroughs in pure mathematics to date.

The Riemann hypothesis carries a $1 million prize from the Clay Mathematics Institute for a correct proof, and countless results in number theory assume it is true without being able to confirm it. Details of exactly how the AI contributed, and whether the proof holds up, have not been independently verified.

As with previous AI maths claims, the result will need scrutiny from the wider mathematical community before being accepted. If confirmed, it would bolster the case that AI reasoning models can meaningfully assist, not just automate, frontier research.

Sources: BBC · WSJ · Business Standard

Key concepts in this story
Riemann hypothesis
The Riemann hypothesis is a 19th-century conjecture about how prime numbers are spread out among all numbers. It's considered one of the most important unsolved problems in mathematics, with a $1 million prize for proving it.
AI reasoning model
A reasoning model is an AI system designed to work through multi-step logical problems rather than just predicting the next likely word. These models are increasingly tested on hard maths and science problems to gauge genuine problem-solving ability.
Peer review
Peer review is the process where other experts check a claimed discovery before it's accepted as valid. Until mathematicians independently verify this proof, the claim remains unconfirmed.
Why it matters for you

Claims like this shape public perception of how capable AI really is, and being able to distinguish a verified result from an unconfirmed claim is a useful critical-thinking skill in any AI-related job.

AI & finance

Bill.com Agrees to $6bn Take-Private Deal with TPG and Corpay

The spend-management platform used by nearly 500,000 businesses will leave public markets in a buyout led by private equity firm TPG.

Bill.com has agreed to be acquired by private equity firm TPG, working alongside payments company Corpay, in a deal valuing the business at around $6 billion. The company describes itself as an 'intelligent finance platform' that helps businesses manage and move money.

The deal would take Bill.com private, removing it from public stock markets where it has traded since its 2019 listing. Corpay, a payments and spend-management specialist, is partnering with TPG on the transaction, suggesting the combined buyers see value in merging Bill.com's small-business tools with Corpay's payments infrastructure.

The agreement is described as definitive, meaning both sides have signed binding terms, though the deal will still need to close through customary regulatory and shareholder approvals.

Sources: Finextra Research Headlines

Key concepts in this story
Take-private deal
A take-private deal is when investors buy up all the shares of a publicly listed company and remove it from the stock exchange, so it's no longer owned by the public. This often happens when buyers believe they can run the company better, or more profitably, outside public market scrutiny.
Definitive agreement
A definitive agreement is a signed, legally binding contract setting out the final terms of a deal, as opposed to an earlier non-binding letter of intent. It signals the deal is locked in, subject to closing conditions like regulatory approval.
Spend management
Spend management refers to software that helps businesses track, control and automate how money flows out of the company, like paying invoices or employee expenses. It's a growing category as companies look to cut financial admin costs.
Why it matters for you

Large fintech buyouts like this show private equity's growing appetite for software that automates financial operations, a trend worth mentioning if you're interviewing for roles in fintech or corporate finance.

Barclays, Citadel and BNY Deploy Anthropic's Claude Across Trading and Operations

Three major financial institutions are reportedly rolling out Anthropic's AI model for tasks spanning research, compliance and operations.

Barclays, hedge fund Citadel, and bank BNY are reportedly putting Anthropic's Claude model to work inside their operations, according to PYMNTS. The specifics of what tasks Claude is handling at each firm were not detailed in available reporting.

The move reflects a broader trend of major financial institutions integrating large language models into everyday workflows, from summarising research to supporting compliance teams, rather than building their own AI systems from scratch. Anthropic, a frontier AI lab, has positioned Claude as a model well-suited to enterprise use cases that demand careful, controllable outputs.

Wider adoption by household-name banks and hedge funds would be a signal of growing institutional trust in commercial AI models for sensitive financial work, though questions around accuracy, data security and oversight typically accompany such rollouts.

Sources: PYMNTS.com

Key concepts in this story
Large language model (LLM)
A large language model is an AI system trained on huge amounts of text to predict and generate human-like language. Claude, like ChatGPT, is an LLM that can answer questions, summarise documents or draft reports when a financial institution asks it to.
Enterprise AI deployment
This means rolling out AI tools inside a company's actual day-to-day operations, not just testing them. For a bank, that might mean using an LLM to help analysts summarise reports faster, with staff checking the output before it's used.
Hedge fund (reminder)
A hedge fund is an investment firm that pools money from wealthy investors or institutions and uses varied, often aggressive strategies to try to generate high returns. Citadel is one of the largest hedge funds in the world.
Why it matters for you

Seeing named banks and hedge funds adopt a specific AI model is a strong signal that fintech and finance employers increasingly expect staff to understand how to work alongside LLMs, a useful point to raise in any finance interview.

Fed Governor Cook Says Central Bank Is Watching How AI Firms Are Financed

A top Federal Reserve official flagged growing attention to the debt and equity funding AI companies, amid broader bubble fears.

Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook said the central bank is closely monitoring how artificial intelligence companies are being financed, according to Reuters. The comment comes amid mounting debate over whether soaring AI valuations and heavy borrowing to fund data centre construction pose risks to the wider financial system.

Central banks like the Fed don't just set interest rates; they also watch for build-ups of risk that could threaten financial stability, such as excessive debt concentrated in one fast-growing sector. AI companies and the infrastructure providers building data centres for them have taken on large amounts of financing recently, some of it unconventional.

Cook's remarks add to a chorus of officials and investors questioning whether AI investment has become overheated, though she stopped short of declaring a crisis. The Fed's attention suggests regulators see AI financing as a sector worth tracking closely going forward.

Sources: Reuters

Key concepts in this story
Federal Reserve
The Federal Reserve, or 'the Fed', is the central bank of the United States, responsible for setting interest rates and monitoring risks to the financial system. Its governors regularly comment on emerging economic risks, including, now, AI financing.
Systemic risk (reminder)
Systemic risk is the danger that trouble in one part of the financial system, like a wave of defaults by AI companies, could spread and damage the broader economy. Regulators watch for this because one firm's collapse can trigger others.
AI bubble (reminder)
An AI bubble refers to the fear that AI company valuations have risen far beyond what their actual profits or prospects justify, similar to the dotcom bubble of the late 1990s. If true, a sharp correction could wipe out significant investor wealth.
Why it matters for you

When a central bank official publicly flags a sector's financing, it's often an early sign that policymakers may eventually tighten scrutiny or rules, something worth watching if you want to work in AI-adjacent finance or policy.

Startups & funding

Coding Startup Cognition in Talks for $75bn Valuation

The AI coding agent maker is reportedly seeking more than $2bn in new funding at a valuation far above its previous level.

Cognition, the startup behind an AI coding agent, is reportedly in talks to raise more than $2 billion in new funding at a valuation of around $75 billion, according to Bloomberg and Reuters. If completed, the round would represent a dramatic jump from the company's earlier valuation.

Cognition's product uses AI agents, systems that can autonomously carry out multi-step coding tasks, to help developers write and fix software faster. The fundraising talks reflect continued investor enthusiasm for companies building specialised, or 'vertical', AI tools aimed at specific professional tasks rather than general-purpose assistants.

Terms of the deal, including which investors are involved, have not been confirmed. A completed round at this scale would place Cognition among the most highly valued AI startups globally, underscoring how quickly valuations in the sector continue to climb.

Sources: Bloomberg.com · Reuters · Unite.AI

Key concepts in this story
AI coding agent
An AI coding agent is a system that can write, test and fix computer code with minimal human guidance, going beyond simple code suggestions to carry out whole tasks. Cognition's main product works this way, aiming to automate chunks of a developer's job.
Funding round (reminder)
A funding round is when a company raises money from investors in exchange for equity, usually in stages such as Series A, B, or later growth rounds. Cognition's reported $2bn-plus raise would be a very large late-stage round.
Up round (reminder)
An up round is when a company raises new money at a higher valuation than its previous round, signalling investor confidence has grown. A jump to $75bn would represent a significant up round for Cognition.
Why it matters for you

Massive valuations for AI coding tools show real demand for automating software development, a trend worth understanding if you're learning to code, since it changes what skills will be valuable for engineers in the coming years.

Startups & funding

Arva AI Raises $20m to Automate Bank Financial-Crime Compliance

The startup's Series A funding will scale AI tools that help banks spot money laundering and fraud faster.

Arva AI has raised $20 million in a Series A funding round to build AI tools that automate financial crime compliance work at banks, according to Wowtale. The company's technology is aimed at helping banks detect and investigate suspicious activity like money laundering more efficiently.

Banks are required by law to monitor transactions for signs of financial crime, a process that traditionally involves large compliance teams manually reviewing flagged activity. Startups like Arva AI are pitching AI systems that can sift through transactions and highlight the riskiest cases, potentially cutting costs and speeding up investigations.

The size of the round places Arva AI among a growing wave of 'vertical' AI startups targeting specific, regulation-heavy corners of the finance industry rather than building general-purpose tools.

Sources: Wowtale

Key concepts in this story
Series A
A Series A is typically a startup's first major round of venture funding after initial seed investment, usually used to grow the product and team once there's early evidence it works. A $20 million Series A is a solid, but not huge, early-stage raise.
Anti-money laundering (AML)
Anti-money laundering refers to the laws and processes banks must follow to detect and report attempts to disguise illegally obtained money as legitimate funds. Compliance teams review suspicious transactions as part of these legal obligations.
Vertical AI (reminder)
Vertical AI means an AI product built for one specific industry or task, rather than a general-purpose tool. Arva AI's focus on bank compliance, rather than AI for everything, is a classic vertical AI approach.
Why it matters for you

Compliance automation is a fast-growing hiring area in fintech, combining regulatory knowledge with AI skills, so understanding terms like AML and vertical AI is useful if you're eyeing roles that bridge finance and technology.

Code corner

Simulating an AI Compliance Risk Check

Based on: Arva AI Raises $20m to Automate Bank Financial-Crime Compliance

Inspired by Arva AI's financial-crime compliance tools, here's a simplified example of how software might process a fake AI response flagging a risky transaction. No real API call is made; we use a pretend response to show the pattern.

import json

# Simulated LLM API response (in real life this would come from an API call)
fake_response = {
    "transaction_id": "TXN1042",
    "description": "Wire transfer to shell company in offshore account",
    "risk_assessment": {
        "risk_level": "high",
        "flags": ["shell_company", "offshore_account"],
        "confidence": 0.92
    }
}

def summarise_risk(response):
    risk = response["risk_assessment"]
    print(f"Transaction {response['transaction_id']}: {response['description']}")
    print(f"Risk level: {risk['risk_level'].upper()} (confidence {risk['confidence']*100:.0f}%)")
    if risk["flags"]:
        print("Flags raised:", ", ".join(risk["flags"]))

summarise_risk(fake_response)

# Convert to JSON string, as an API would send it
json_payload = json.dumps(fake_response, indent=2)
print("\nRaw JSON payload:\n", json_payload)
Line by line
  1. `import json` brings in Python's built-in library for reading and writing JSON, the text format most APIs use to send structured data.
  2. `fake_response` is a dictionary standing in for what a real AI compliance API might return after analysing a transaction, with nested details under 'risk_assessment'.
  3. `def summarise_risk(response):` defines a function that takes any response dictionary and prints a human-readable summary.
  4. Inside the function, `risk = response["risk_assessment"]` pulls out the nested dictionary so we can access its fields more easily.
  5. The f-strings build formatted output lines, including converting the confidence score from a decimal (0.92) to a percentage (92%).
  6. `", ".join(risk["flags"])` turns the list of flags into a single comma-separated string for display.
  7. Finally, `json.dumps(fake_response, indent=2)` converts the dictionary back into a neatly formatted JSON string, similar to what you'd see in a real API response.

Try it: Change the risk_level to 'low', clear the flags list to an empty list [], and lower the confidence to 0.3, then rerun the code to see how the summary changes. Can you add a new flag, like 'rapid_succession_transfers', and update the printed output accordingly?
