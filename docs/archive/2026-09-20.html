Trump dismisses AI safety fears as 'hoax', unveils plans for an 'AI Force'

The US president wants a new name for artificial intelligence and a military-style unit to oversee it, but offered no detail on either.

President Donald Trump used his social media platform on Saturday to claim that growing concern about artificial intelligence is a political hoax cooked up by Democrats, comparing it to past controversies over Russia and climate change. He also launched a poll asking followers to help rename AI, offering options such as 'Superior Intelligence' and 'Supreme Intelligence', and announced he would soon reveal an 'AI Czar' and form a new 'AI Force', modelled on the Space Force he created during his first term.

Trump's comments arrive amid a wider industry argument about how seriously to take the risks of advanced AI. A researcher recently quit Anthropic, saying leading labs privately fear their technology could prove catastrophic within the decade. Anthropic's chief executive, Dario Amodei, responded with a plan to 'pace the frontier' of development, an approach that both OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk appeared to endorse. Meanwhile Nvidia's chief executive Jensen Huang backed Trump's 'hoax' framing at a recent tech conference, insisting the industry would not allow itself to be slowed down.

No details have been given on what the AI Force or the promised AI czar role would actually do, and Trump did not present evidence for his hoax claim. Critics argue that framing safety concerns as fake distracts from more immediate issues, such as AI's environmental footprint and its effect on jobs. With no policy specifics yet, the announcement is being read largely as political theatre, though it does confirm that Washington sees AI oversight as a live turf war.

Sources: TechCrunch · DW.com

Key concepts in this story
AI alignment
Reminder: this is the effort to make sure an AI system's goals and behaviour actually match what humans intend, rather than pursuing a target in unexpected or harmful ways.
Frontier AI lab
A company building the most advanced, largest AI models at any given time, such as OpenAI, Anthropic or Google DeepMind.
Data centre backlash
Growing public and political resistance to new AI data centres, often over their electricity use, water consumption or effect on local communities.
Why it matters for you

Political rhetoric about AI safety shapes the regulatory environment that AI companies, and anyone building products on top of their models, will have to operate in. If you're aiming for a policy, compliance or comms role in AI, being able to explain the difference between genuine safety debate and political messaging is a useful interview skill.

China's central bank adviser warns AI boom could deepen oversupply

A People's Bank of China adviser cautioned that heavy AI investment risks worsening the mismatch between production and weak consumer demand.

An adviser to the People's Bank of China has warned that the country's rapid build-out of AI infrastructure could make an existing economic problem worse: too much supply chasing too little demand. China has already been grappling with soft consumer spending, and pouring investment into chips, data centres and AI services risks adding capacity without a matching rise in demand.

The warning reflects broader unease among Chinese policymakers that AI, while a strategic priority, could exacerbate deflationary pressure if factories and tech firms keep expanding output while households remain cautious about spending.

Sources: Reuters · Межа. Новини України. · the420.in

Key concepts in this story
Supply-demand imbalance
This happens when an economy produces more goods and services than people are willing or able to buy, which can push prices down and squeeze company profits.
Deflationary pressure
A trend towards falling prices across an economy, which sounds nice for shoppers but can discourage investment and wages, since businesses expect to earn less in future.
Why it matters for you

Understanding how AI investment interacts with a country's broader economic health is useful if you ever analyse AI stocks or emerging-market risk, since a slowdown in China's consumption could ripple through global chip demand.

Huawei brings forward Ascend 960 AI chip to challenge Nvidia by 2027

Huawei has reportedly moved up the launch timeline for its next-generation AI chip as China pushes for semiconductor self-sufficiency.

Huawei is reportedly accelerating the rollout of its Ascend 960 AI chip, now targeting a 2027 launch, as it aims to close the gap with Nvidia in the Chinese market. The move comes as Chinese firms face restrictions on buying the most advanced Nvidia chips, pushing domestic alternatives higher up the priority list.

Details of the chip's specifications have not been confirmed, but the accelerated timeline signals how seriously Chinese chipmakers are treating the race to reduce reliance on foreign AI hardware.

Sources: korben.info · The Daily Star

Key concepts in this story
GPU (graphics processing unit)
Reminder: a GPU is a chip originally built for video game graphics that turns out to be very good at the maths behind training and running AI models, because it can do many calculations at once.
Export controls
Reminder: these are government rules restricting which technologies, like advanced chips, can be sold to certain countries, often for national security reasons.
Why it matters for you

Chip competition between the US and China affects who can access the most powerful AI hardware, which in turn shapes which companies and countries can train the biggest models — a useful thread to follow if you're interested in AI hardware or geopolitics roles.

AI & finance

OpenAI reportedly set to burn through $280bn in cash by 2030

A Financial Times report suggests OpenAI's spending on compute and expansion will far outstrip its revenue for years to come.

OpenAI is reportedly on track to burn through close to $280 billion in cash by the end of the decade, according to the Financial Times. The figure underlines how expensive it is to train and run frontier AI models, even for the company widely seen as the industry leader.

No further detail on the breakdown of these costs was available, but the scale of the number has reignited debate about whether current AI revenues can ever catch up with the enormous cost of compute, data centres and staff needed to stay competitive.

Sources: Seeking Alpha

Key concepts in this story
Cash burn
The rate at which a company spends its cash reserves before it starts generating enough revenue or profit to sustain itself, like a phone battery draining faster than it charges.
Revenue forecast
Reminder: an estimate of how much money a company expects to earn in future, used by investors to judge whether current spending is sustainable.
Why it matters for you

Big cash-burn numbers are central to the 'AI bubble' debate, and being able to talk about burn rate versus revenue is a sharp way to show financial literacy in any fintech or analyst interview.

AI & finance

Economists debate whether AI hype or government debt is the bigger market risk

Commentators are increasingly asking whether rising Treasury yields, not an AI bubble, pose the real threat to stock markets.

A wave of commentary this week, including from CEPR and Goldman Sachs, has questioned whether calls to slow down AI development are missing the bigger picture: rising government debt and Treasury yields. As yields rise, borrowing becomes more expensive across the economy, which can make it harder to justify the high valuations many AI-linked stocks currently carry.

No single consensus has emerged, but the discussion reflects growing nervousness that stock market gains built partly on AI optimism could be vulnerable if borrowing costs keep climbing.

Sources: cepr.net · Beansprout · The Dark Side Of The Boom

Key concepts in this story
Treasury yields
The return investors get for lending money to the US government by buying its bonds; when yields rise, it usually means borrowing costs are rising across the whole economy too.
AI bubble
The idea that AI company valuations have risen further than their actual business performance justifies, similar to the dot-com bubble of the late 1990s.
Why it matters for you

Linking AI valuations to macroeconomic factors like interest rates is exactly the kind of connection that impresses interviewers in finance roles, since it shows you can see beyond the tech headlines to the underlying economics.

Oracle's $18bn data centre debt draws scrutiny from short seller Michael Burry

Local opposition to data centre projects and questions over financial disclosures are putting pressure on Oracle's AI cloud spending.

Oracle's roughly $18 billion in debt tied to data centre expansion is reportedly facing pressure from local opposition to new projects, according to NewsBytes. Separately, investor Michael Burry, known for betting against the 2008 housing market, has questioned Oracle's financial disclosures amid wider scrutiny of Big Tech's AI spending.

The Motley Fool has asked whether Oracle's debt load could ultimately derail its ambitions to become a major AI cloud provider, though no verdict has been reached. Together, the reports point to growing investor unease about how much debt tech giants are taking on to fund AI infrastructure.

Sources: NewsBytes · Crypto Briefing · The Motley Fool

Key concepts in this story
Data centre lease
Reminder: an agreement where a company rents space and power capacity in a data centre rather than building its own, a common way AI firms scale up quickly.
Junk bond
Reminder: a bond issued by a company seen as higher risk, which must pay a higher interest rate to attract lenders because there's a bigger chance it won't repay in full.
Why it matters for you

Watching how much debt AI infrastructure companies take on, and how sceptics like Burry scrutinise their accounts, is a good habit for anyone wanting to work in equity research or credit analysis.

Dutch chip startup Euclyd raises $231m with Samsung backing

The AI chip startup's funding round, backed by Samsung, adds to a wave of investment chasing alternatives to established chipmakers.

Euclyd, a Dutch AI chip startup, has raised $231 million in a funding round backed by Samsung, according to reports. Few further details of the round's structure or the company's technology have been disclosed.

The investment reflects continuing investor appetite for companies trying to challenge established players in AI hardware, an area where demand has surged alongside the wider AI boom.

Sources: Tech Edition · RS Web Solutions

Key concepts in this story
Funding round
Reminder: a stage at which a startup sells a slice of ownership to investors in exchange for cash to grow the business, for example raising $231 million in return for company shares.
Strategic investor
An investor, like Samsung here, who puts money into a startup partly because it fits their own business interests, not purely for financial return.
Why it matters for you

Chip startups attracting large sums shows there's still appetite to fund competitors to Nvidia and Huawei, which matters if you're considering hardware, semiconductors or venture capital as a career path.

Stripe reportedly pursuing $10bn acquisition of OpenRouter while weighing PayPal move

Payments giant Stripe is said to be eyeing the AI model marketplace OpenRouter in a deal that sent PayPal shares lower.

Stripe is reportedly pursuing a $10 billion acquisition of OpenRouter, a platform that lets developers access many different AI models through a single service, according to Stocktwits. News of the potential deal coincided with a roughly 2% after-hours drop in PayPal's share price, as Stripe is also said to be weighing its next move regarding PayPal.

No official confirmation of the acquisition terms has been given, and details remain limited. If completed, the deal would deepen Stripe's presence in AI infrastructure, alongside its existing payments business.

Sources: Stocktwits

Key concepts in this story
Acquisition
Reminder: when one company buys another outright, usually to gain its technology, customers or team rather than build the same thing from scratch.
Model marketplace / API aggregator
A service like OpenRouter that lets developers switch between different AI models (from different companies) through one shared connection point, similar to a price-comparison site for AI models.
Why it matters for you

A payments company buying an AI model marketplace shows how fintech and AI infrastructure are merging, which is a strong talking point if you're pitching yourself for roles at the intersection of the two industries.

Code corner

How fast is the cash draining? A simple burn-rate calculator

Based on: OpenAI reportedly set to burn through $280bn in cash by 2030

OpenAI is reportedly set to burn nearly $280 billion by 2030. Let's build a tiny Python tool that estimates how long a company's cash reserves would last given a monthly burn rate, the kind of quick sanity check an analyst might run.

def months_of_runway(cash_reserves, monthly_burn):
    if monthly_burn <= 0:
        return float('inf')
    return cash_reserves / monthly_burn

def describe_runway(cash_reserves, monthly_burn):
    months = months_of_runway(cash_reserves, monthly_burn)
    years = months / 12
    return f"At a burn rate of ${monthly_burn:,.0f}/month, ${cash_reserves:,.0f} lasts about {months:.1f} months (~{years:.1f} years)."

# Example: a company with $50bn cash burning $4bn a month
cash = 50_000_000_000
burn = 4_000_000_000
print(describe_runway(cash, burn))

# Try a lower burn rate
print(describe_runway(cash, 1_500_000_000))
Line by line
  1. def months_of_runway(...) defines a function that divides total cash by monthly spending to find how many months it will last.
  2. The check for monthly_burn <= 0 avoids a division-by-zero error if burn rate is somehow zero or negative.
  3. describe_runway() calls that function and formats the result into a readable sentence, converting months into years too.
  4. The :,.0f formatting adds comma separators and rounds to whole numbers, so $50000000000 prints as $50,000,000,000.
  5. The two example calls show how changing only the burn rate dramatically changes the runway, mirroring how investors worry about AI companies' spending pace.

Try it: Change the cash and burn variables to model OpenAI's reported $280bn total burn spread evenly over the years from 2026 to 2030, and calculate the average monthly burn rate implied.
