The AI industry

Nvidia lines up $2bn of European AI deals, starting with four French startups

Chief executive Jensen Huang is set to announce fresh investments in Europe within days, including stakes in health-tech firm Doctolib.

Nvidia is preparing to announce around $2bn worth of deals across Europe over the coming week, according to multiple reports, with chief executive Jensen Huang set to unveil new partnerships and investments. Among the first confirmed moves, Nvidia has taken minority stakes in four French technology companies, including the health-booking platform Doctolib and robotics firm Genesis AI.

Nvidia makes the graphics processing units, or GPUs, that power most of today's large language models, and it has increasingly used its enormous cash pile to invest directly in the companies that use its chips. These strategic stakes let Nvidia both back promising startups and ensure they remain loyal, chip-hungry customers, rather than simply selling hardware at arm's length.

The French push fits a broader pattern of Nvidia planting flags across Europe, where governments are keen to build local AI champions rather than depend entirely on American and Chinese technology. For Nvidia, spreading investments across many startups and countries also diversifies its exposure beyond the handful of giant US labs it has already backed heavily.

With more announcements expected this week, markets will be watching whether Nvidia's spending spree extends beyond France to other European hubs, and whether these smaller bets can generate the kind of returns its stakes in larger US labs have delivered.

Sources: Bloomberg.com · Reuters · BigGo Finance

Key concepts in this story
Strategic stake
This is when a big company buys a minority share in a smaller one not just for profit, but to build a closer business relationship, such as guaranteeing a loyal customer for its products.
GPU (graphics processing unit)
A GPU is a chip originally built for rendering video game graphics that turns out to be extremely good at the maths behind training and running AI models, making it the core hardware of the AI boom.
Why it matters for you

If you're hunting for AI jobs in Europe, these deals signal where funding and hiring will concentrate next, particularly in health-tech and robotics. In an interview, you can point to this as evidence that chipmakers are now also acting like venture investors.

FBI arrests OpenAI engineer after company says it used AI to catch him planting a bug

A hidden recording device found wrapped in toilet paper behind a conference room TV led to the arrest of a 35-year-old Saudi employee.

OpenAI says it discovered a hidden recording device plugged into a television in one of its executive conference rooms, where highly confidential technical and commercial discussions take place. The company says it used its own AI models to help trace the device back to a specific employee, Abdulrahman Alfozan, who was arrested by the FBI on Friday and charged with illegally intercepting oral communications.

According to the Department of Justice, the device was installed around 28 September and its contents were encrypted. CCTV footage allegedly showed Alfozan near the room on several occasions before the device was found, including appearing to look behind the TV where it was hidden. Investigators also say they found a second recording device and his ID in a bag left elsewhere in the office.

OpenAI says it has no evidence the employee accessed or stole model weights, source code or customer data, and that he has since been fired. The case lands as OpenAI and its rivals face growing scrutiny over security and safety practices, given how sensitive and commercially valuable their internal research has become.

The FBI's investigation is ongoing. The episode is likely to be cited as an example of AI tools being turned towards corporate security and insider-threat detection, a use case companies are increasingly keen to advertise.

Sources: The Verge · Tech · Reuters

Key concepts in this story
Insider threat
This refers to security risks that come from people who already have legitimate access to a company, such as employees, rather than outside hackers.
Inference
Inference is what happens when a trained AI model is actually used to answer a question or analyse data, as opposed to the earlier process of training it; OpenAI says it ran its models at inference time to help identify the culprit.
Why it matters for you

This story shows AI labs are now using their own products for internal security work, a growing niche combining cybersecurity and AI skills that's worth mentioning if you're aiming for a security or trust-and-safety role at a tech company.

ASML says chipmaking customers are already booking orders for 2029 and 2030

The Dutch lithography giant beat profit forecasts and raised its 2026 sales outlook as AI-driven chip demand keeps outrunning supply.

ASML, the only maker of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines used to print the world's most advanced chips, said on Wednesday that some customers are already expressing interest in placing orders for 2029 and 2030. The comment came alongside third-quarter results that beat analyst expectations, with net profit of €4.96bn against a forecast of €4.1bn.

Lithography is the process of using light to etch tiny circuit patterns onto silicon wafers, and ASML's EUV machines are so advanced that firms such as TSMC and Samsung depend on them to make the chips behind today's AI boom. Chief executive Christophe Fouquet said planned supply for 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet customer demand, and the company is looking at ways to expand capacity further.

ASML has now raised its full-year guidance three times this year and expects 2026 sales of up to €45bn, with shares up 56% so far this year. Both Samsung and TSMC committed during the quarter to using ASML's next-generation High NA EUV tool, which analysts see as central to the firm's future growth.

ASML reiterated that China will remain around 20% of its business, even as it remains barred from selling its most advanced EUV machines there. A rival Chinese firm has reportedly made progress on less advanced lithography tools, but analysts say it remains far behind ASML's technology.

Sources: Tech · Global Banking & Finance Review

Key concepts in this story
Lithography
Lithography is the process chipmakers use to print microscopic circuit patterns onto silicon wafers using light; more advanced lithography lets you pack more transistors into the same space.
Extreme ultraviolet (EUV)
EUV is a very short wavelength of light used in the newest lithography machines to etch finer, smaller circuits than older methods allow, which is essential for making today's fastest AI chips.
Why it matters for you

Orders booked years in advance are a strong signal that the AI infrastructure buildout isn't slowing, which matters if you're assessing whether chip and AI stocks are in a bubble, a theme running through several of today's stories.

CoreWeave seeks 'unlimited' insurance funding to bankroll its AI data centres

The cloud computing firm is reportedly exploring insurance-backed financing to fund more AI infrastructure building.

CoreWeave, a cloud computing company that rents out GPU capacity for AI training and inference, is reportedly targeting what it calls 'unlimited' insurance money to help finance its rapid data centre expansion, according to the Financial Times. Details of exactly how this financing would work have not been fully disclosed.

CoreWeave has already relied heavily on debt financing to build the vast data centres needed to run AI workloads, and insurance-backed funding would be another way to raise large sums without issuing new shares or taking on conventional bank loans.

This fits a broader trend of AI infrastructure firms searching for increasingly creative sources of capital, as the cost of building data centres packed with GPUs continues to climb and investors grow more cautious about traditional debt markets.

How exactly insurers would price the risk of lending against AI infrastructure, and what happens if demand for computing power slows, remains an open question worth watching as more details emerge.

Sources: Financial Times

Key concepts in this story
Insurance-linked financing
This is when a company raises money by effectively getting insurers to back a loan or bond, using the insurer's balance sheet or risk appetite rather than a bank's, often to access larger sums.
AI infrastructure
This refers to the physical backbone of AI, including data centres, chips and power supply, needed to train and run large models at scale.
Why it matters for you

Novel financing structures like this are a sign that traditional lenders may be reaching limits on how much AI infrastructure debt they want to hold, which connects directly to today's story about memory chip stocks wobbling over AI financing worries.

Micron and memory chip stocks tumble again as AI financing fears deepen

SK Hynix and Samsung shares sank more than 7% after a Chinese rival claimed a breakthrough in memory chip technology.

Shares in memory chipmakers fell sharply for a second straight day, with Micron down 3.6%, SK Hynix plunging more than 10%, and Samsung Electronics losing 7.4%, both hitting their lowest levels since August. The sell-off followed news that China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) had made progress on a new chip architecture that packs more data into less space.

Memory chips, including high-bandwidth memory (HBM) used in AI systems, are dominated by Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron, with CXMT seen as a distant challenger still struggling to match their advanced manufacturing. Analysts said the scale of the sell-off looked overblown given CXMT has not yet proven it can produce at scale, but said it reflected how nervous investors have become about the broader AI trade.

The declines spread globally, hitting ASML, Infineon and STMicroelectronics in Europe, and SoftBank, Tokyo Electron and Advantest in Japan, as comments from Elon Musk about China's manufacturing scale added to the unease. The chief executives of Intel and Broadcom have separately warned that access to capital could become a bigger constraint on the AI boom than actual chip demand.

Investors are now watching a busy week of earnings and US inflation data for clues on interest rates, which tend to move inversely with growth stocks like chipmakers. The episode underlines how sensitive AI-linked shares have become to any hint of competitive or financing risk, even when underlying demand for chips remains strong.

Sources: Tech

Key concepts in this story
High-bandwidth memory (HBM)
HBM is a specialised type of memory chip that sits very close to a processor and transfers data extremely fast, making it essential for AI chips that need to move huge amounts of information quickly.
Market concentration
This means a small number of companies control most of a market; because just three firms dominate advanced memory chips, bad news about any one of them can shake the whole sector's stock prices.
Why it matters for you

This volatility shows how a single competitor claim can wipe billions off AI-linked shares, a useful example if you're asked in an interview to explain why AI stocks are considered risky despite strong underlying demand.

Bank of America fined $1.2bn over anti-money laundering failures

Regulators penalised the bank for shortcomings in systems meant to detect and prevent illicit financial flows.

Bank of America has been hit with a $1.2bn fine after regulators found failings in its anti-money laundering (AML) controls, according to the BBC. Further details of the specific failings have not yet been reported.

Anti-money laundering rules require banks to monitor transactions and flag suspicious activity that could involve criminal proceeds being disguised as legitimate funds. Large fines like this are a recurring feature of banking regulation, reflecting how seriously authorities treat compliance failures even at major institutions.

The penalty adds to a long list of AML-related fines faced by big banks in recent years, underscoring the scale of investment banks must make in compliance technology and staff to avoid similar penalties.

Sources: BBC

Key concepts in this story
Anti-money laundering (AML)
AML rules require banks to check where customers' money comes from and report suspicious transactions, to stop criminals disguising illegally obtained cash as normal business income.
Civil money penalty
This is a fine imposed by a regulator, rather than a criminal court, usually for breaking rules like AML requirements without necessarily proving criminal intent.
Why it matters for you

Compliance and AML roles remain a steady source of entry-level jobs at big banks, and this fine is a reminder that financial institutions are increasingly turning to AI tools to automate transaction monitoring and reduce exactly this kind of regulatory risk.

Startups & funding

Amazon reportedly nears $7bn deal for AI startup Decart after Anthropic walks away

The three-year-old startup makes software that speeds up AI workloads on custom chips, reportedly outbidding a deal with rival lab Anthropic.

Amazon is reportedly in advanced talks to acquire Decart, an AI startup specialising in software that makes AI models run faster on chips, in a deal worth around $7bn according to the Wall Street Journal. Decart, founded only three years ago, had reportedly been in discussions with Anthropic before Amazon entered the picture.

The deal would reportedly help Amazon improve the speed and efficiency of AI workloads running on its own custom chips, reducing its reliance on Nvidia's GPUs. Amazon has been investing heavily in building its own chip and AI infrastructure capabilities to compete with rivals like Microsoft and Google.

If completed, the acquisition would be one of the largest AI startup deals of the year and would add to a wave of major tech companies buying up specialist AI firms rather than building every capability from scratch.

Terms have not been finalised and the deal could still change, but it reflects the scale of money being thrown at companies that can squeeze more performance out of existing AI chips.

Sources: Trak.in · The Business Journals · Global Cosmetics News

Key concepts in this story
Acquisition
An acquisition is when one company buys another outright, taking full ownership, as opposed to simply investing in a minority stake.
Valuation multiple
This is a way of judging how expensive a deal is relative to a company's size, such as comparing the $7bn price tag to Decart's revenue or user base to see if it looks cheap or expensive.
Why it matters for you

Big tech companies buying three-year-old AI startups for billions shows how much value is placed on specialised software skills around chip efficiency, a useful niche to highlight if you're learning about both AI and hardware.

Workday to acquire agentic identity startup C1.ai

The HR software giant is buying a startup that manages digital identities for both humans and AI agents.

Workday has agreed to acquire C1.ai, a startup that builds identity management tools designed to work for both human employees and AI agents, according to reports. Terms of the deal have not been disclosed.

As companies deploy more AI agents, software that can act semi-independently to complete tasks like booking travel or processing invoices, they need new ways to control who, or what, is allowed to access which systems and data. C1.ai's platform is designed to extend traditional identity management, which already governs human logins and permissions, to cover these AI agents as well.

The acquisition fits Workday's push into enterprise AI governance, as large companies look for ways to deploy AI agents safely without losing control over sensitive data or systems. It also reflects a broader trend of software firms buying smaller AI governance startups rather than building these capabilities from scratch.

The deal is expected to strengthen Workday's offering to large enterprise customers who are increasingly worried about managing security and compliance risks as AI agents take on more autonomous tasks within their organisations.

Sources: The Business Journals · Yahoo Finance · Unite.AI

Key concepts in this story
Agentic identity
This is the idea of giving an AI agent its own verified digital identity and permissions, similar to a human employee's login, so companies can track and control what it's allowed to access.
Enterprise AI
Enterprise AI refers to AI tools built specifically for use inside large companies, often focused on tasks like HR, finance or compliance rather than consumer products.
Why it matters for you

As more companies deploy AI agents, identity and access management for AI is becoming a fast-growing niche worth knowing about if you're interested in enterprise software or AI governance careers.

Code corner

Tracking a memory-chip portfolio sell-off

Based on: Micron and memory chip stocks tumble again as AI financing fears deepen

Today's memory chip sell-off hit several stocks by different amounts. Here's a quick way to calculate the overall loss on a simple portfolio using only Python's standard library.

portfolio = {
    "Micron": {"shares": 50, "price": 120.0, "pct_change": -3.6},
    "SK Hynix": {"shares": 20, "price": 250.0, "pct_change": -10.2},
    "Samsung": {"shares": 30, "price": 70.0, "pct_change": -7.4},
}

total_before = 0.0
total_after = 0.0

for name, data in portfolio.items():
    value_before = data["shares"] * data["price"]
    value_after = value_before * (1 + data["pct_change"] / 100)
    loss = value_before - value_after
    print(f"{name}: lost ${loss:,.2f} ({data['pct_change']}%)")
    total_before += value_before
    total_after += value_after

total_loss = total_before - total_after
print(f"\nTotal portfolio value before: ${total_before:,.2f}")
print(f"Total portfolio value after:  ${total_after:,.2f}")
print(f"Total loss: ${total_loss:,.2f} ({total_loss/total_before*100:.2f}%)")
Line by line
  1. We store each stock's number of shares, price, and percentage change in a dictionary of dictionaries, so each company's data stays grouped together.
  2. `value_before = data["shares"] * data["price"]` calculates how much that holding was worth before the sell-off.
  3. `value_after = value_before * (1 + data["pct_change"] / 100)` applies the percentage change; dividing by 100 converts something like -3.6 into -0.036, and adding 1 keeps most of the value while subtracting the loss.
  4. We accumulate `total_before` and `total_after` across the loop so we can work out the overall portfolio loss at the end, not just each stock individually.
  5. The final f-string formats numbers with commas and two decimal places using `:,.2f`, which is a handy trick for displaying money clearly.

Try it: Add a fourth stock, such as Western Digital with its own shares, price and percentage change, and re-run the code to see how it affects the total portfolio loss.
