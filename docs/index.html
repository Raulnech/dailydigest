The AI industry

OpenAI slashes funding target to $20bn as investor appetite cools

ChatGPT's maker has reportedly pared back its latest fundraising goal from $30 billion, with only $11 billion secured so far.

OpenAI has lowered the target for its newest funding round to around $20 billion, down from an earlier goal of $30 billion, according to reports from the Financial Times and Wall Street Journal. The first close of the round — the point at which an initial batch of investor money is locked in — is expected to land even below that reduced figure.

The round had previously been discussed at eye-watering valuations of $1.4 trillion and then $1.25 trillion, as OpenAI prepared the ground for a future stock market listing, known as an IPO. Reports suggest a group of investors from the United Arab Emirates will lead the round, with a separate offering aimed at wealthy individual investors potentially topping up the total to the $20 billion mark. So far, only $11 billion has reportedly been committed, and large investors have apparently given a lukewarm response.

The timing is awkward: Wall Street has grown nervous about the sheer scale of spending on AI data centres and chips, triggering a sell-off in technology stocks. Rival lab Anthropic is also said to be facing a bumpier path to its own planned listing. If a company as prominent as OpenAI is struggling to hit its fundraising targets, it raises a bigger question — whether investors are starting to doubt that today's AI spending will pay off.

Sources: TechCrunch · Tech · Investor's Business Daily

Key concepts in this story
Funding round
A funding round is a batch of money raised from investors in exchange for a stake in a company. A 'first close' is simply the point where some of that money is locked in, even before the whole round is finished.
IPO (initial public offering)
An IPO is when a private company sells shares to the public for the first time, letting ordinary investors buy in and existing investors cash out.
Valuation
A company's valuation is what investors think it's worth, usually calculated as share price times number of shares. OpenAI's valuation has been discussed at over a trillion dollars, even though it isn't yet publicly listed.
Why it matters for you

If you're job-hunting in AI or fintech, this is a sign that even the biggest names face tougher fundraising conditions — a useful thing to mention in interviews about market awareness. It also links directly to today's bond-market and neocloud stories about whether AI spending is sustainable.

The AI industry

Nvidia's $12.93bn Hugging Face deal draws antitrust and fraud scrutiny

Nvidia's purchase of AI model-sharing platform Hugging Face is facing competition concerns just as regulators eye AI hype more broadly.

Nvidia's reported $12.93 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, a popular online hub where developers share and download AI models, has drawn antitrust scrutiny, according to reports. Antitrust review is the process regulators use to check whether a deal would let a company unfairly dominate a market — in this case, concerns centre on Nvidia's already-dominant position in AI chips extending into the software layer where models are shared and run.

Separately, reports note that U.S. regulators are watching AI companies more closely for signs that bold public claims about AI capabilities or revenue could cross the line into securities fraud — the crime of misleading investors to affect a company's share price. With AI valuations under pressure, as seen in OpenAI's funding troubles, regulators appear increasingly alert to companies overstating their prospects.

Together, the two stories point to AI firms facing growing regulatory pressure from multiple directions — on market dominance and on truthfulness to investors — just as enthusiasm for AI stocks shows signs of cooling.

Sources: ad-hoc-news.de · Coinpaper

Key concepts in this story
Model hub
A model hub is an online platform, like Hugging Face, where developers upload and download pre-trained AI models to use or build on, similar to an app store but for AI.
Securities fraud
Securities fraud happens when a company or its executives mislead investors — for example exaggerating revenue or AI capabilities — in a way that affects the share price.
Antitrust review (reminder)
Antitrust review is when regulators check whether a merger or acquisition would give a company too much power in a market, potentially harming competition and consumers.
Why it matters for you

Understanding antitrust and securities law basics is increasingly useful for careers in AI policy, compliance, or finance roles at tech firms, and it's a good way to show interviewers you follow regulatory risk, not just product news.

Alphabet's chip unit reportedly draws OpenAI and Foxconn interest as cash flow turns negative

OpenAI and contract manufacturer Foxconn are said to be exploring hardware tie-ups as Alphabet's free cash flow dips into the red.

Reports suggest OpenAI could be interested in Alphabet's chip-making business, while Foxconn, the Taiwanese contract manufacturer best known for assembling iPhones, is separately exploring a new partnership with OpenAI on AI hardware. The reports come as Alphabet's free cash flow — the cash a business has left after running itself and investing in new equipment — has reportedly turned negative, a sign of how much money is being poured into AI infrastructure.

Alphabet has developed its own custom AI chips, known as TPUs, as an alternative to buying from Nvidia. Interest from a major AI lab like OpenAI would suggest growing appetite for diversifying away from a single chip supplier, while a Foxconn tie-up could help OpenAI move further into building physical hardware rather than just software.

Details remain thin, and neither company has confirmed the reports. But the pattern fits a broader trend: AI labs increasingly want more control over the chips and hardware that power their models, rather than relying entirely on outside suppliers.

Sources: ad-hoc-news.de · ET Datacenters

Key concepts in this story
Custom chip (TPU)
A TPU, or Tensor Processing Unit, is a chip Alphabet designed specifically to run AI calculations efficiently, as an alternative to general-purpose GPUs made by companies like Nvidia.
Contract manufacturer
A contract manufacturer builds physical products on behalf of another company — Foxconn assembles iPhones for Apple, for example, rather than designing them itself.
Free cash flow (reminder)
Free cash flow is the money left over after a company pays its running costs and invests in equipment. Negative free cash flow means a company is spending more than it's bringing in, often because of heavy investment.
Why it matters for you

Chip supply chains are becoming a major career area — from procurement to hardware engineering — and tracking who partners with whom helps you understand where power is shifting in the AI stack.

KB Kookmin Bank leads Korea's first $9.45bn GPU financing deal

The South Korean bank is arranging a record syndicated loan to fund AI chip purchases, reportedly the country's largest of its kind.

KB Kookmin Bank is set to lead a financing deal worth roughly $9.45 billion (reported elsewhere as up to 12.6 trillion won) to fund the purchase of GPUs — the specialised chips used to train and run AI models — for South Korean AI infrastructure. Reports describe it as the country's first and largest syndicated loan dedicated to GPU financing.

A syndicated loan is a large loan provided jointly by a group of banks, rather than a single lender, spreading the risk among several institutions. GPU-backed loans use the chips themselves, or the contracts to use them, as collateral, a financing model that has become increasingly common globally as AI firms race to secure enough computing power.

The deal underlines how AI infrastructure spending is now significant enough to require coordinated bank lending even outside the United States, and puts South Korean lenders squarely in the middle of the global AI build-out — and its financial risks.

Sources: The Korea Times · 파이낸셜뉴스 · Chosunbiz

Key concepts in this story
Syndicated loan
A syndicated loan is a big loan split between several banks working together, so no single lender has to take on all the risk. It's common for financing deals too large for one bank to handle alone.
GPU-backed loan (reminder)
A GPU-backed loan uses graphics processing chips, or the revenue contracts tied to them, as collateral — security the lender can claim if the borrower fails to repay.
Data centre (reminder)
A data centre is a building full of servers and chips that run AI models; GPU financing deals like this one typically fund the hardware inside them.
Why it matters for you

This shows banking and credit roles increasingly require understanding AI infrastructure economics — a useful talking point if you're interviewing for corporate banking, credit analysis or fintech roles in Asia.

Lloyds shares slide despite record nine-month profit

Lloyds Banking Group posted its best nine-month profit on record but its shares fell as the bank's 2027 outlook dimmed.

Lloyds Banking Group reported record profit for the first nine months of the year, yet its shares fell, reportedly because the bank's guidance — its own forecast of future performance — for 2027 looked weaker than investors had hoped.

This is a common pattern in markets: a company's results can beat expectations for the current period while its share price still drops, if investors think future growth will be slower. For a bank like Lloyds, future profit depends heavily on factors such as interest rates, loan demand and the broader health of the UK economy.

Investors will be watching closely for more detail on what's dragging down the bank's medium-term outlook, especially as rising global uncertainty around AI-related market swings adds to pressure on bank share prices generally.

Sources: Foreign Policy Journal

Key concepts in this story
Forward guidance
Forward guidance is a company's own prediction about its future profits or performance, which investors use to judge whether to buy or sell its shares.
Net profit
Net profit is what a company earns after subtracting all its costs, taxes and expenses from revenue — the 'bottom line' figure investors pay close attention to.
Share price reaction
A share price reaction is how a stock moves after news is released; it reflects whether results were better or worse than what investors had already expected, not just whether they were good in absolute terms.
Why it matters for you

Understanding why good results can still send a share price down is a classic interview topic for finance and investment roles, showing you grasp market expectations rather than just headline numbers.

Bond market and 'neocloud' warnings cloud the AI rally

Analysts say bond investors and little-known AI infrastructure firms called neoclouds are both flashing caution signs for the AI-driven stock boom.

Commentary from the Motley Fool suggests the bond market — where governments and companies borrow money by issuing debt — is sending a more cautious signal about AI infrastructure spending than the booming stock market has reflected. Rising borrowing costs or widening credit spreads, the extra interest lenders demand for riskier borrowers, can be an early warning that investors are nervous about repayment risk.

Separately, the BBC reports that 'neoclouds' — lesser-known companies that rent out AI computing power, often funded heavily by debt — are fuelling fears that the AI investment boom could be a bubble. These firms typically borrow heavily to buy chips and data centre capacity, betting on continued demand from AI labs.

Together, the stories suggest that while AI stocks have surged, parts of the financial system funding that surge — bonds and heavily leveraged infrastructure firms — are showing more caution, a divergence worth watching closely.

Sources: The Motley Fool · BBC

Key concepts in this story
Bond market
The bond market is where governments and companies borrow money from investors by issuing debt, promising to repay with interest. It often reacts to risk more conservatively than the stock market.
Credit spread
A credit spread is the extra interest rate a riskier borrower must pay compared with a safer one, like a government. A widening spread suggests lenders see more risk of not being repaid.
Neocloud (reminder)
A neocloud is a newer company that rents out AI computing power, often built up using heavy borrowing to buy chips and data centre capacity.
Why it matters for you

Spotting the gap between what stock markets and bond markets are signalling is a valuable analytical skill for finance careers, and ties directly into today's OpenAI funding story about investor caution.

Startups & funding

Salesforce to buy defence startup Rune for reported $375m

The enterprise software giant is reportedly acquiring a defence-focused AI startup, extending its push into government work.

Salesforce is reportedly acquiring Rune, a startup focused on defence technology, for around $375 million, according to Dealroom. Details of what Rune's products specifically do have not been widely confirmed, but its focus on defence suggests the deal is about winning more government and military customers for AI-powered software.

Big tech and software companies have increasingly been acquiring smaller, specialised 'vertical AI' startups — companies that build AI tools tailored to one industry — rather than building every capability from scratch. Buying into defence tech can also mean navigating sensitive government contracts and security requirements that differ significantly from typical commercial software sales.

If confirmed, the deal would mark another example of a major enterprise software company betting that bolt-on acquisitions, rather than in-house development, are the fastest route into fast-growing, specialised AI markets.

Sources: Dealroom

Key concepts in this story
Defence tech
Defence tech refers to technology built specifically for military or government security uses, such as surveillance, logistics or battlefield AI tools, which often require special government contracts and clearance.
Government contract
A government contract is an agreement where a company is paid by a government agency to supply goods or services, often involving strict rules, long approval processes and high scrutiny.
Acquisition (reminder)
An acquisition is when one company buys another, typically to gain its technology, customers or talent faster than building those things in-house.
Why it matters for you

Defence AI is a growing, well-funded niche within the industry — worth knowing about if you're considering specialised AI careers, since it often requires different skills like security clearance and compliance awareness.

Startups & funding

Mercor faces claims of 4TB data leak

The AI hiring startup Mercor is reportedly facing claims that 4 terabytes of data were exposed in a breach.

Reports from TechJuice claim that Mercor, a startup that uses AI to match workers with jobs, has suffered a data breach involving around 4 terabytes of data — a huge volume, roughly equivalent to millions of documents or images. The scale and nature of the leaked data, and how it happened, have not been independently confirmed.

Mercor operates as a 'vertical AI' company, building AI specifically for recruitment and hiring rather than general-purpose use. Startups handling large amounts of personal data, such as resumes, work history or identity documents, are attractive targets for attackers because that information can be valuable for fraud or identity theft.

If confirmed, the breach would raise fresh questions about how carefully fast-growing AI startups are protecting the sensitive data they collect, especially as more hiring and financial decisions increasingly rely on AI systems trained on personal information.

Sources: TechJuice

Key concepts in this story
Data breach
A data breach is an incident where private or sensitive information is accessed or exposed without authorisation, often by hackers, which can put affected individuals at risk of fraud.
Vertical AI (reminder)
Vertical AI refers to AI products built for one specific industry, like hiring or healthcare, rather than general-purpose tools — Mercor focuses specifically on recruitment.
Personally identifiable information (PII)
PII is any data that can identify a specific person, such as a name, address or ID number. Leaks of PII are particularly serious because they can enable identity theft.
Why it matters for you

Data security is a growing concern wherever AI handles personal information, creating demand for cybersecurity and data governance roles — a good angle to raise if asked about risks of AI adoption in an interview.

Code corner

Working out interest on a syndicated GPU loan

Based on: KB Kookmin Bank leads Korea's first $9.45bn GPU financing deal

KB Kookmin Bank is leading a roughly $9.45 billion syndicated loan to finance GPU purchases. Here's a simple way to estimate how much interest would build up on a loan like that over time, using nothing but Python's standard library.

def loan_interest(principal, annual_rate, years):
    """Calculate simple interest and total repayment."""
    interest = principal * annual_rate * years
    total_repayment = principal + interest
    return interest, total_repayment

# Example: part of Kookmin's syndicated GPU loan
principal = 9_450_000_000  # $9.45 billion, in dollars
annual_rate = 0.06          # 6% annual interest rate (example figure)
years = 5

interest, total = loan_interest(principal, annual_rate, years)

print(f"Principal: ${principal:,.0f}")
print(f"Interest over {years} years: ${interest:,.0f}")
print(f"Total repayment: ${total:,.0f}")
Line by line
  1. def loan_interest(...): defines a reusable function that calculates simple interest, meaning the interest doesn't compound each year.
  2. interest = principal * annual_rate * years applies the simple interest formula: loan amount times rate times number of years.
  3. total_repayment = principal + interest adds the interest on top of the original loan to find what must eventually be repaid.
  4. The f-strings with :,.0f format large numbers with commas and no decimal places, making billions easier to read.
  5. Changing annual_rate or years instantly shows how sensitive total repayment is to borrowing costs — exactly the kind of risk banks assess before joining a syndicate.

Try it: Try changing annual_rate to 0.08 to see how a higher interest rate changes the total repayment, or write a loop that prints the running interest owed at the end of each year instead of just the final total.
